Educational Review

Interactive Recap & Concept Check

Test your understanding of the core inference-time scaling principles demonstrated above.

Prompt 1
After increasing inference effort, what changed: success rate, compute cost, or both?
Concept: Increasing effort increases both success rate and total search compute cost. However, returns diminish: the gain from LOW to MEDIUM (+56.7% on Easy) is substantially larger than the gain from MEDIUM to HIGH (+10.0%), meaning each additional unit of compute buys progressively less accuracy.
Prompt 2
How is BDH-CQ's effort mechanism different from this live N-Queens demo?
Concept: This live demo scales discrete search computation (independent random restarts and iterative min-conflicts repair steps on a constraint puzzle). In contrast, BDH-CQ scales recurrent latent-state computation (recurrent associative working-memory updates inside a trained neural sequence model without verbalized reasoning tokens). They represent two distinct mechanisms that exhibit the same general accuracy-versus-compute scaling trade-off.
Experimental Scope & Methodological Disclosures
  • Fixed Effort Presets: Compute budgets are calibrated fixed presets: LOW (1 restart × 20 steps), MEDIUM (3 restarts × 25 steps), HIGH (5 restarts × 40 steps).
  • Sample Size & Noise: Live experiment metrics are computed over 30 independent trials per setting; minor statistical variance between seeds is genuine to the randomized local search.
  • Algorithmic Analogy, Not Reproduction: N-Queens under min-conflicts is a classic constraint satisfaction problem, not a language model. It illustrates the general mathematical phenomenon of inference-time compute scaling, not BDH-CQ's proprietary model weights or training.
  • Difficulty Ceiling: On Hard (24-Queens), increasing effort to HIGH elevates success to ~80% but does not reach 100%, demonstrating that test-time scaling raises achievable performance ceilings but cannot guarantee perfection on arbitrarily difficult instances.
