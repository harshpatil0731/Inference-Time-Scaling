The Central Claim

More compute does not always mean proportionally better performance.

When modern reasoning models and constraint solvers allocate additional compute during inference, they explore wider solution branches and repair conflicting partial states.

However, this trade-off is governed by an asymmetric law: initial investments in compute resolve the vast majority of solvable states, while subsequent compute yields progressively smaller accuracy returns at exponentially higher computational expense.

Initial Budget Surge: Transitioning from minimal to balanced compute yields the steepest performance surge (+56.7%). Diminishing Plateau: High budgets saturate reachable states; each marginal percentage requires exponentially more steps (+10.0%). Complexity Ceiling: On genuinely hard problems (24-Queens), increasing compute raises the ceiling (~80%) but cannot guarantee perfection.

Law of Diminishing Returns

The steepest performance leap occurs during the initial transition from minimal exploration to balanced search budget (+56.7% from LOW to MEDIUM on Easy 8-Queens). Beyond this sweet spot, pushing to HIGH yields merely +10.0% additional accuracy while multiplying search step consumption by 2.6×.

System Architecture

Methodological Execution Pipeline

Every metric, trajectory, and visualization is computed deterministically in your browser using pure algorithmic execution.

1

Configuration Presets

Select an effort budget (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH) and problem size (Easy N=8, Hard N=24). Sets restart limit and maximum step threshold.

2

Seeded PRNG

Linear congruential generator (LCG) initializes queen placements with strict deterministic seeds (42..82), guaranteeing 100% reproducible trials.

3

Min-Conflicts Repair

Heuristic local search selects conflicting queens and relocates them to rows with minimal pairwise attacks until solved or budget exhausted.

4

boardTrace Logging

Every queen move, conflict count, and restart event is captured frame-by-frame directly by the solver engine into an immutable execution log.

5

SVG Trace Replay

Interactive chessboard renders authentic solver frames sequentially with conflict heatmaps, step stepping, and live speed controls.

6

Pareto Analysis

Marginal return differentials (ΔS / ΔC) and empirical Pareto frontiers are calculated live to identify optimal compute allocations.

Experimental Scope & Methodological Disclosures
  • Fixed Effort Presets: Compute budgets are calibrated fixed presets: LOW (1 restart × 20 steps), MEDIUM (3 restarts × 25 steps), HIGH (5 restarts × 40 steps).
  • Sample Size & Noise: Live experiment metrics are computed over 30 independent trials per setting; minor statistical variance between seeds is genuine to the randomized local search.
  • Algorithmic Analogy, Not Reproduction: N-Queens under min-conflicts is a classic constraint satisfaction problem, not a language model. It illustrates the general mathematical phenomenon of inference-time compute scaling, not BDH-CQ's proprietary model weights or training.
  • Difficulty Ceiling: On Hard (24-Queens), increasing effort to HIGH elevates success to ~80% but does not reach 100%, demonstrating that test-time scaling raises achievable performance ceilings but cannot guarantee perfection on arbitrarily difficult instances.
Educational Review

Interactive Recap & Concept Check

Test your understanding of the core inference-time scaling principles demonstrated above.

Prompt 1
After increasing inference effort, what changed: success rate, compute cost, or both?
Concept: Increasing effort increases both success rate and total search compute cost. However, returns diminish: the gain from LOW to MEDIUM (+56.7% on Easy) is substantially larger than the gain from MEDIUM to HIGH (+10.0%), meaning each additional unit of compute buys progressively less accuracy.
Prompt 2
How is BDH-CQ's effort mechanism different from this live N-Queens demo?
Concept: This live demo scales discrete search computation (independent random restarts and iterative min-conflicts repair steps on a constraint puzzle). In contrast, BDH-CQ scales recurrent latent-state computation (recurrent associative working-memory updates inside a trained neural sequence model without verbalized reasoning tokens). They represent two distinct mechanisms that exhibit the same general accuracy-versus-compute scaling trade-off.
